# Frontier Engineering Challenge 2026

## Overview
**Build at the frontier of agentic AI. August 28–31, 2026. Online, individual, free.**
AI can produce convincing code in seconds. Real engineering begins when convincing is not enough: incomplete requirements, hidden dependencies, difficult edge cases, failure modes, and decisions that require technical judgment.

The micro1 Frontier Engineering Challenge is a free, global, online competition for engineers ready to work at that frontier. Over a three-day sprint you will use coding agents to tackle a real-world software engineering problem and produce a solution that is correct, reproducible, testable and clearly explained.

### Process
1. Register.
    - Free, takes two minutes. Registration closes Aug 29 at 23:59 UTC.
2. Build.
    - Problem released at kickoff. Three days, coding agents required.
3. Prove.
    - Show a baseline and an improved solution, with evidence.
4. Submit.
    - Code, reproduction guide, video and agent trajectories.

The full challenge statement is released at kickoff (Aug 28 at 15:00 UTC). Until then, expect to build at the frontier of agentic AI without details that would reveal the problem in advance.

Participants will be able to registre after the Hackathon kicks off. The Hackathon ends on Aug 31 at 18:00 UTC.

Participants are evaluated on the quality of the submitted work, not résumé prestige, job title, university, employer or geography.

Coding-agent use is required. You must disclose the tools you used and submit the required trajectories for evaluation.

## Evaluation Criteria
**Scored out of 100 by micro1's engineering team.**

**Qualification gate. A submission is scored only after it passes eligibility, completeness, integrity, trace and reproducibility checks. A project that cannot be run or verified may be disqualified before rubric scoring.**

| Criterion                   | Weight | What judges assess                                                                                           |
|-----------------------------|--------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Problem & user value        | 15%    | Solves a meaningful problem for a clearly defined user. Who experiences the bottleneck, and why it matters.  |
| Agent solution & engineering| 30%    | Uses agents purposefully and is technically sound. Which design choices helped the agent solve the problem?  |
| End‑to‑end quality          | 20%    | Completes a realistic, self‑contained execution and produces a result the user can use.                       |
| Measured improvement        | 15%    | Shows gains over a fair baseline and uses the changelog to connect each iteration to evidence.               |
| Reproducibility             | 15%    | Provides a clear path for another person to run the solution and baseline from a clean environment.          |
| Hot take / insights         | 5%     | Turns an observed failure mode into a practical lesson for building more reliable agents.                    |

### Tie-break order
1. Higher Agent Solution & Engineering score
2. Higher Reproducibility score
3. Higher Measured Improvement score
4. Higher End to End Quality score
5. Final panel review of documented evidence. The judges' decision is binding to the extent permitted by law and the official terms


## Submission Package
Four items. All four are required.
1. Complete solution code & improvement changelog
2. Reproduction guide
3. Solution video, up to 5 minutes
4. Agent trajectories

### Complete solution code and improvement changelog
Share the full project and everything required to run it, including the instructions that shape each agent. Use the README to introduce the intended user, explain their current bottleneck, and describe why solving it is valuable. Add a clearly labelled Improvement Changelog with an entry for every meaningful iteration, connected to the evidence that guided your next decision. Close with the main failure mode and your hot take.

### Reproduction guide. 
Written for someone starting from a clean environment. Walk them through setup and give the exact commands for the solution, the baseline and the evaluation. Explain which data is required and what output to expect. Share relevant versions along with approximate runtime and cost.

### Solution video, up to 5 minutes. 
Begin with the problem and the simple baseline, then walk through one realistic execution from start to finish. Show the final comparison and briefly explain the changelog. Highlight the change that contributed most, and one experiment you removed.

### Agent trajectories. 
Representative trajectories for every agent you used, easy to follow from the agent instructions through to the final result. Show what the agent did and how its tools responded. Capture the feedback that shaped its next step, plus any retries or human checkpoints.

## Rule Book
1. You are welcome to build with tools and components you already know.
2. Make it clear what existed before the competition and what you added.
3. Use every tool and component according to its licence and service terms.
4. Keep consequential actions controlled through a sandbox or simulation. Add human approval before the action happens.
5. Make a qualified human reviewer part of any solution that could significantly affect someone.
6. Choose a legal and ethical use case that treats people and their data responsibly.
7. Use information you are allowed to share. Public or synthetic data are usually easiest. Approved anonymous data also works.
8. Keep credentials and private information outside the submission.
9. Connect every claim about your results to the evidence you submit.
10. Give judges enough access to run the project and reproduce the main result.

**Submissions are governed by the Hackathon Participation Agreement (accepted at registration), under which micro1 owns submissions and may use them for AI model training and evaluation.**
